In the top-ten technique, the prioritisation is performed by a number of various stakeholders, who are responsible for selecting their own top ten requirements [5,13]. These techniques (numerical assignment, top-ten and priority group and ranking) are suitable for a small dataset of requirements and have inabilities and/or disadvantages in dealing with large-scale requirements, specifying the relative priority value of each requirement and catering the SQP process to evaluate participating stakeholders [5,13].
