## STEPS IN CONDUCTING A RESEARCH PROJECT
1. Identification of a practical problem - Motivation; (optional)
2. Identification of a research problem
3. Literature review
4. Determining specific research questions / Specification of a conceptual framework
5. Choice of a research methodology
   - Data collection
   - Verifying data
   - Analysing and interpreting the data
6. Implementation of the chosen research method steps...
7. Reporting and evaluating research / Communicating the research findings and recommendations

## RESEARCH QUALITY CRITERIA

- **Originality:** Are the RQ/conclusions novel? Never reached before?
- **Relevance:** Are the RQ/conclusions of interest/value for the research community (or broad society)?
- **Validity:** Are the conclusions right?
- **Reproducibility:** Can the conclusions be reproduced/repeated?

*Irina Rychkova, CRI, University Paris 1*

## VALIDATION OF RESULTS: THREATS TO VALIDITY

Are the results trustworthy? Meaningful?

- **Internal validity:** How well the study is conducted (controls extraneous variables, eliminates alternative explanations).
- **External validity:** How the findings can be generalised to the real world / practical situations.
- **Validity Threats:** Factors that can compromise internal or external validity.
- **Mitigation strategies:** Actions taken to minimize threats and improve validity.

*Irina Rychkova, CRI, University Paris 1*

---

## INTERNAL VS. EXTERNAL VALIDITY

| Internal Validity | External Validity |
| :--- | :--- |
| Conclusions are warranted | Findings can be generalized |
| Controls extraneous variables | Outcomes apply to practical situations |
| Eliminates alternative explanations | Results apply to the world at large |
| Focus on accuracy and strong research methods | Results can be translated into another context |

*Irina Rychkova, CRI, University Paris 1*

---

## OTHER TYPES OF VALIDITY TO CONSIDER:
- **Conclusion validity:** Is there a relationship between the cause and outcome?
- **Construct validity (theoretical validity):** Do measurements reflect the theoretical concepts?
- **Experimental validity:** Precision of experimental setups.
- **Descriptive validity:** Accuracy in reporting observations.

## REPORTING RESEARCH: ETHICS AND PLAGIARISM

### Definition of Plagiarism:
> "To steal and pass off (the ideas or words of another) as one's own: to use (another's production) without crediting the source." *(Merriam-Webster)*

> "Plagiarism involves the intentional or negligent use, by paraphrase or direct quotation, of the published or unpublished work of another person without full and clear acknowledgment in all such scholarly work..." *(Bowdoin Academic Honor Code)*

*Irina Rychkova, CRI, University Paris 1*

---

## PLAGIARISM EXPLAINED
- Can happen accidentally
- Applies even if you paraphrase/translate
- Using other sources is good, as long as you cite them
- Citing sources shows which ideas are your own

---

## TYPES OF PLAGIARISM
- **Global plagiarism:** Plagiarizing an entire text.
- **Verbatim plagiarism:** Directly copying someone's words without quotation marks or citation.
- **Paraphrasing plagiarism:** Rephrasing someone else's ideas and presenting them as your own.
- **Patchwork plagiarism:** Copying phrases/passages from different sources and combining them.
- **Translation plagiarism:** Translating a text from another language without citing the source.
- **Self-plagiarism:** Recycling previous work that you have already submitted or published.

*Pay strict attention to Referencing, Quotation, Paraphrasing, and Translation.*

*Irina Rychkova, CRI, University Paris 1*

---

## CONSEQUENCES OF PLAGIARISM
- Grade penalties
- Failing your course
- Disciplinary action
- Suspension or expulsion

---

## HOW TO AVOID PLAGIARISM?

1. **Keep track of all sources:** Articles, books, websites, videos, whitepapers, blog posts, etc.
2. **Quote and paraphrase properly.**
3. **Cite your sources in the text systematically.**

---

## SOURCE DOCUMENTING = CITATION
Required bibliographic data for complete source documentation:
- Author name
- Source title
- Publication date
- Publisher
- Page numbers of specific quotes
- URL or DOI for online sources
- Access date

---

## QUOTE VS. PARAPHRASE

| Technique | How? | When to use? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Quoting** | Copy exact words, use quotation marks, cite source.  <br>*Ex:* According to Darwin, "..."(1859, p. 510). | To give textual evidence, analyze language, or state precise definitions. |
| **Paraphrasing** | Put text into your own words, no quotation marks, cite source. <br>*Ex:* According to Darwin, natural selection is... (1859, p. 510). | To support/illustrate claims, explain complex ideas, show understanding. |

> **Rule:** Quoting $> 30$ words $\rightarrow$ need to paraphrase!

---

## TRANSLATION RULES (ENGLISH TO FRENCH)
- Use paraphrasing and citations when you translate concepts from English into French.
- You can use direct quotation in English in your French text if exact phrasing is critical.

## IN-TEXT CITATION & BIBLIOGRAPHY STYLES

### Author-Date (e.g. APA):
- **In-text:** Recent research has shown that plagiarism is an issue (Smith & Thomas, 2018, pp. 34-36).
- **Bibliography:** Smith, T. H., & Thomas, L. (2018). *New challenges in higher education*. Free Press.

### Numeric (e.g. IEEE / Vancouver):
- **In-text:** Recent research has shown that plagiarism is an issue [1].
- **Bibliography:** [1] Smith, T. H., & Thomas, L. (2018). *New challenges in higher education*. Free Press.

## SCIENTIFIC REPORT STRUCTURE (INDICATIVE)

- **Abstract**
- **1. Introduction** (~5%) - Problem setting
- **2. Background** (~5%)
- **3. Research methodology** (~10%)
- **4. Solution / Results** (~35%) - Data collection & Data analysis
- **5. Analysis / Discussion of results** (~25%) - Validation
- **6. Related works** (~10%)
- **7. Conclusion / Future work** (~10%)
- **Bibliography**
- **Appendices**
